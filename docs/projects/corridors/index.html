Research / Transition corridors
Exploratory study

Spatially coherent transition corridors

Connecting local expression geometry along physical tissue structure to describe continuous molecular-state variation.

Research question

How can local molecular-state changes be assembled into continuous spatial transitions across tissue?

Approach

Local expression geometry supplies unoriented transition axes. Physical tissue connectivity constrains how these axes are joined into corridors, while each cell or spot's expression supports its position along the continuous state coordinate.

Current evidence

Exploratory implementations and tissue analyses examine continuous transitions alongside anatomical references. The inferred axes describe spatial state variation and do not by themselves identify temporal or causal direction.
